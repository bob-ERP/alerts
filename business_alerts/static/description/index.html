Business Alerts

Configurable alert scenarios on any Odoo model — count and time-based conditions evaluated on a schedule, delivered to an n8n webhook or by email

Production Ready LGPL-3 Odoo 19.0 🌐 itsbob.co GitHub
Overview

Operational problems in Odoo usually announce themselves as data: stuck queue jobs, orders without a delivery, an import that stopped arriving. Business Alerts lets an administrator describe such conditions declaratively — a model, a domain, a threshold — as alert scenarios. A scheduled job evaluates all enabled scenarios and, when one triggers, posts a JSON payload to a webhook (built for n8n) or sends a template email. Triggered alerts suspend themselves to avoid alarm storms and can be re-armed manually or by a second scheduled job.

Features
🔢

Count Alerts

Trigger when the number of records matching the domain reaches the Trigger Value — e.g. "≥ 1 queue job in error for over an hour". Domains support datetime expressions, evaluated via safe_eval.

⏱️

Time Alerts

Trigger when the age of the last matching record (by any date/datetime field, e.g. create_date) exceeds a threshold in seconds, minutes, hours, days or years — "no new Shopify order in 6 hours".

🔗

Webhook Delivery (n8n)

POSTs a JSON payload — alert name, message, company, severity, deep link into the matching Odoo view, and the alert owner's name / email / phone — to any webhook URL. Email delivery via a mail.template is available as an alternative.

🔕

Self-Suspending, Self-Re-Arming

A fired alert switches itself to Suspended so it doesn't fire on every cron tick. The Reset Suspended Alerts job (or a manual click) re-enables it. Every send is logged in the scenario's chatter.

⚙️

Cron Control from Settings

Both scheduled jobs (evaluation & reset) are switched on/off and re-timed directly from Settings → Business Alerts — no digging in Scheduled Actions.

🧪

Tested

Automated tests cover count and time triggering (webhook mocked, payload asserted), the below-threshold case, the suspend/reset cycle and missing-URL safety.

Configuration

Scenarios are managed in the Business Alerts app; the evaluation schedule lives under Settings → Business Alerts (system administrators only).

Field Description
Model / Domain Which records to watch: any ir.model plus a record domain (records_domain).
Alert Function Count (with value threshold) or Time (with time_frame, time_unit and a date/datetime time_field_id).
Delivery Webhook (with webhook_url) or Email (with mail_template_id); plus Severity, Alert Owner and the window Action used to build the deep link.
Settings toggles alerts_evaluation and reset_suspended_alerts with their interval number/unit — writing them reconfigures the two ir.cron records.
Evaluation Flow

1. The Business Alerts: Alerts cron runs cron_trigger_alert_jobs() on all Enabled scenarios

2. Each scenario's domain is safe_eval'd (with a sandboxed datetime) and its condition checked — record count vs. threshold, or age of the newest matching record

3. On trigger, send_alert() POSTs the JSON payload (or sends the email), the result is logged to the chatter, and the scenario flips to Suspended

4. The Reset Suspended Alerts cron re-enables suspended scenarios on its own schedule

Technical Information

Models

alert.scenario New model (inherits mail.thread, mail.activity.mixin) holding the scenario definition, with process_alerts(), send_alert() and both cron entry points.
res.config.settings Interval toggles stored as ir.config_parameters; set_values() writes them onto the two cron records.
